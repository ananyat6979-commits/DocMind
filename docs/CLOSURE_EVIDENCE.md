# Bounded calibration, robustness and infrastructure evidence

This follow-up closes implementation and measurement gaps within named profiles. It does not relabel historical results, validate the hosted provider or finish the separate Hindi PDF claim-label work.

## Calibration

The [document-disjoint HotpotQA component](../reports/releases/r3/calibration/README.md) completed 128 calibration and 128 held-out questions after a ten-question timing pilot. Provided article titles and paragraph hashes are disjoint across all selected questions. The grid, signals, error targets and family correction were frozen before held-out outcomes. Every frozen request completed. None of the six fixed-grid policies qualified at 10 or 20 percent exact-match error.

Token-confidence AUROC was 0.757 [0.655, 0.851]; the separate verification prompt was 0.562 [0.495, 0.623]. Those are nominal bootstrap ranking intervals with different feature-availability counts, not a paired improvement or calibrated release guarantee. The verifier assigned probability 1.0 to 96 held-out candidates, of which 64 failed exact match. This is disagreement with exact match, not established factual overconfidence: verbose answers and aliases can fail EM while remaining correct. The human study below found substantial disagreement and has not adjudicated it. Fresh experiments score an explicit short-answer field and stratify answer length. Public benchmark contamination in model training is not assessed, exact match can miss aliases, and a coarse frozen grid does not exclude every possible alternative thresholding method.

The zero-release policy entails 128 review decisions for 128 held-out requests. This is workload, not completed human annotation. The [completed single-reviewer study](../reports/releases/r3/review_cost/README.md) has all 27 study labels plus two practice items. Elapsed time was 53.96 minutes; the reviewer estimated at least 45 minutes spent reviewing with interruptions. The legacy 20-minute timer stopped while labeling continued, so 16 study-item timings are censored and only 11 are conservatively usable. No layout time-saving or population cost is claimed. Twenty-three answers were marked correct, three incorrect and one unclear; 17 decisive judgments disagree with exact match and remain unadjudicated. This cannot validate Hindi attribution or general staffing cost. Future packets enforce the cap and preserve unfinished items.

## Robustness

The [broader reader/policy component](../reports/releases/r3/robustness/README.md) completed 180 synthetic cases across six questions, ten instruction families, two poisoning families and three benign controls under paired observation serializations. Current JSON serialization produced 1/60 contaminated injection candidates and 0/60 contaminated automatic releases; raw text produced 4/60 and 2/60. Only ten injection families were tested, so family uncertainty is wide. The observed improvement is narrow and exploratory. One direct numeric override still reached the current candidate, and raw role-spoofing passed NLI and citations twice.

This suite uses the actual pinned FP16 local reader and ReAct loop, fixed retrieval and the English NLI judge. It does not test hostile retrieval at corpus scale, cross-language attacks, arbitrary tool misuse or real-secret exfiltration. Parser isolation and API authorization have separate behavioral checks. They are not language-model attack success rates.

## Persistent service profile

The [opt-in PostgreSQL profile](POSTGRES_DEPLOYMENT.md) adds persistent transactional pgvector indexing, explicit tenant scopes, forced row policies, shared bounded quotas, per-tenant upload roots and renewable ingestion leases. Runtime credentials are separate from migration credentials. Corpus transactions reject stale leases; expired claims can be recovered by another worker. Synchronous upload and deletion use the authenticated tenant store. Newly generated responses cannot record already deleted sources. Operational counters are scoped to the current tenant and worker.

Live CI covers isolation for documents, chunk prefixes, neighbors, vectors, jobs, reviews and events; tenant-header spoofing; concurrent version updates; shared quotas across two API processes; terminated claimant recovery; and expiry during a transaction. A quota backend outage fails closed. A separate real-model HTTP profile verifies retrieval and stored NLI execution rather than inferring neural work from HTTP status. The [two-process real-model HTTP runs](../reports/releases/r3/infrastructure/README.md) each completed 24/24 warm requests and 26 NLI executions including cold calls. Their p95 latencies were 1498 and 1856 ms at concurrency four, with demo generation and one small synthetic document. Both runs remain published. Load timing remains distinct from hosted generation.

A 50,000-paragraph CPU scale attempt timed out during embedding before any document writes. Its [failure record](../reports/releases/r3/scale/initial_cpu_timeout.json) is retained. The unchanged [completed retry](../reports/releases/r3/scale/README.md) used a 70-minute step and 85-minute job ceiling on free CPU CI. It persisted 50000 real E5 paragraph vectors and measured exact-neighbor Recall@10 of 95.2% [92.8, 97.3] over 100 queries. All 800 concurrency-sweep searches completed. At concurrency four, database-search throughput was 89.71 requests/s with p95 52.29 ms; concurrency sixteen reached 100.26 requests/s with p95 193.78 ms. Query embeddings were precomputed, so these are not HTTP or neural-reader rates. A real database restart and fresh-process search passed. The result is separate from historical Gaussian-vector and smaller-corpus experiments.

The checked-in Compose target is one host with two workers and a shared private upload volume. Multi-host object storage, regional failover, operator backup schedules and external availability remain outside its evidence. A synthetic same-cluster dump/fresh-database restore passed, preserving vector bytes, pending reviews, queued jobs, events and tenant denials. Passing CI is not proof of an operating production deployment.

## Reproducibility and cost

Source revisions, model revisions, dependency identities, requested denominators, failure records and numeric predictions are published for the two completed GPU components. Model-free consistency checks run in CI; they are not an independent external replication. Human packets, raw labels, restricted source text and private review notes remain outside Git. No new hosted evaluation calls, paid annotation or paid compute were used. The exact free-account snapshot after both sessions completed was 3504.539 used seconds with zero active reservations; rounding upward and counting both allocated devices bounds cumulative use at 7080 GPU-seconds against the 21600-second ceiling. The repeated scale run uses free CPU CI.


## Closure boundary

The bounded calibration, broader robustness and persistent-backend work is implemented, measured and independently checked. Negative findings remain part of the result: no calibrated policy qualified, one current-serialization candidate was contaminated, the review timer censored durations, and ANN search missed some exact neighbors. The evidence establishes neither review-time savings nor hosted accuracy, arbitrary injection resistance or production availability. Hindi PDF claim support and a balanced English/Hindi real-reader cohort remain separate unfinished work. About 24.38 minutes remain in the aggregate PDF/review budget after conservatively charging this overrun; the public-label audit has its separate budget.

The [post-hoc answer-length sensitivity](../reports/releases/r4/calibration_answer_length_sensitivity.json) separates concise and verbose full answers and high-verifier-score errors. It changes no historical score, threshold or factual label. Fresh experiments use a linked short-answer field to avoid treating output verbosity as answer content.
