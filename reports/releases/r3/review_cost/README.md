# Single-reviewer cost and answer-quality component

One reviewer completed all 27 sampled study items and two practice items. The packet hid reference answers, routing decisions and experimental names. Questions came from the frozen English held-out component, sampled across token-confidence quartiles plus missing-score cases. Layouts were assigned within matched evidence-length pairs; they used different questions, not a repeated-answer crossover.

The Start-to-Export interval was 3237.689 seconds, or 53.96 minutes. The reviewer reported at least 45 minutes spent reviewing with unquantified interruptions. This is a self-report plus a recorded elapsed interval, not a precise active-time measurement. The requested study cap was 20 minutes. The reviewer continued after it, and the legacy page allowed labels while its timer stopped. That protocol deviation is retained, not rounded away.

Only 11 nonpractice items have conservative usable per-item durations before the cap; 16 are censored, including the final partially timed item. Fifteen finished items had zero recorded duration after the timer stopped. These are missing times, not instantaneous reviews. Early-subset median recorded durations were 95 seconds for six full-source items and 84 seconds for five ordered/highlighted items. This time-selected subset cannot establish a layout advantage or general staffing cost. No time-saving percentage or population cost projection is reported.
